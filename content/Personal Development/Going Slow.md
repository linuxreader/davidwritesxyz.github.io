---
title: Going Slow
description: Why trying to move too fast will keep you from moving anywhere at all.
---

Trying to move too fast will hinder your results. You need time to digest things. So slow down and enjoy the process.

When you move too fast with weight loss, building a business, getting a degree, etc. . 

You become results focused. 

You put yourself in a state of, "when I accomplish this, I will be happy". In this state, happiness never comes. When you do finally achieve your goal..

**It's not enough.** 

You automatically set a new goal and are dissatisfied on the path to get there. 

And that's **if** your speed doesn't crash you into a wall before you even arrive.

Instead, approach your goals from a state of gratitude. Commit yourself to the process but be satisfied by how far you've come. 

## Real world example: My Fitness Journey

I recently got back into fitness. I've put on a little bit of muscle. And lost about 10 lbs of fat. I should have appreciated how much better I already looked and felt. Instead, I fell into the state of, "when I lose 30 more lbs and have a 6 pack, then I will be satisfied with my fitness." 

So I started cutting the calories more. I started training harder. I started getting upset whenever the scale didn't show me a weight that was lower than the day before. (Even though I knew that my daily scale weigh-ins were just part of the process to help me determine if what I was doing was working.)

Moving too fast was getting painful. I was committing more and more time to getting results. I was giving up other important areas of my life. 

**Moving fast was fueling burnout.** 

The ironic thing is, I stopped getting results. The process started getting too hard. And I was becoming Mr. Cranky Pants.

It's important to stretch your comfort zone. You can't grow without some stress. But there is a fine balance that must be maintained. One way to do this is by slowing down. 

With my weight loss example, instead of pushing to lose 2lbs of fat each week, I could aim for 1lb per week. I would still get great results. All while avoiding the burnout that stalls my progress.

If you try to move too fast, you may end up going nowhere at all. 

## So don't have BIG goals at all? 

I'm not saying that you shouldn't have **BIG** aspirations. It's more like, "life is a marathon, not a sprint". If your goal is to achieve something fast, that is fine too. You may just need to set your expectations right. Realize that the faster you move in one area, the more you will have to give up in others. 

If you want balance in life, keep your goals **BIG** but expand your time frame. Set a realistic process that includes small things you can do every day to reach that goal. Commit to the process and forget the rest. 

The rest of your mental time and energy will be free to focus on other things.
