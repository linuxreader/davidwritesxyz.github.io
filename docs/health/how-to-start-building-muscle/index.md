If I were you, I'd build up a base with the most basic compound movements and ignore everything else for a while. Basic compounds are as follows:

1. Squat; Bodyweight squats may be easiest to start with before moving to barbell squats or some other weighted version.
    
2. Hinge: A deadlift is an example of a hinging movement. Deadlifts work your posterior chain (Glutes, hammies, and lower back). Hinges can be a bit hard to do with bodyweight exercises only.
    
3. Horizontal press: Works chest, triceps, serratus, etc. Pushups are great for you and will keep you busy for a while. Then you can advance to bench press, dips, and other weighted movements.
    
4. Horizontal pulls: Horizontal pullups (a pull-up that looks like an upside down pushup.) This will work biceps, back, etc. After bodyweight gets boring you can move to barbell rows, pendlay rows, dumbell rows, etc.
    
5. Vertical pulls: Pullups and their many varieties. Works back, lats, biceps, etc. You can also do lat pulldowns or other machine stuff but pull-ups are the coolest so just get good at those.
    
6. Core: There are different types of core work but I'll lump them all here for brevity. Some great exercises for core are: hanging leg raises, ab wheel rollouts, cable crunches, pallof press, and copenhagen planks.
    

So there are 6 fundamental movements to start with. I know you said "bodybuilding" specifically which focuses on muscular hypertrophy or growing your damn muscles. Hypertrophy comes with your muscles working so hard that they adapt to meet the demands you are putting them through. This means you needs to put in some work. Push yourself until your muscles physically give up. Do this over and over for a long period of time, and eat enough of, and the right kind of food and your muscles will grow.

How often should you lift?

This really depends on your goals, what you are willing to sacrifice in the rest of your life, your recovery, sleep, etc. You could lift anywhere from 2-6 times per week. I usually do 3-4 because the program I am on specifically targets that frequency. In general though, you'll want to hit each muscle group 2-3 times per week for best results. My 4 day a week looks like this:

Monday: Squat, Hanging leg raises, deadlifts

Tuesday: Bench, Horizontal pull-ups, Overhead press

Wednesday: rest, go for a swim, do mobility work, etc.

Thursday: Deadlift, Lunges, ab wheel rollouts

Friday: Overhead press, pull-ups, push-ups

Saturday-Sunday: rest, go for a jog, do mobility work, yard work, etc.

What to do when lifting?

In the beginning, focus on proper form above all else. You can learn any exercise on YouTube. During a workout you can alternate sets of each lift for the day for a certain amount of reps. A superset is a rotation of 3 different lifts. Each set you do can range from 5-30 reps. I suggest building up to 30 reps per set as a beginner before you add weights. This will really give you practice on your form. Then after a month or two, start adding more and more weight until you are in the 8-10 range. You can build muscle anywhere between 5-30 reps but higher reps means you are in the gym longer and it's harder to get to failure.

Rest 1-5 minutes between each set making sure to document rest times, weight and reps of everything you do. You can increase the amount of sets, exercises, weight you do as you see fit.

Above all, focus on getting stronger. That is what is going to drive results.

Diet:

Gonna be honest with you here. You probably need to get an app like Cronometer and learn how to track your food. This will help you figure out what is in your food, so that you can manipulate your body in the way you want. There are some basics that can get you pretty far. But I recommend tracking to anyone. It will open your eyes and be a huge tool for the rest of your life.

To get started:

3-6 cups of veggies per day

3-6 servings of fruit per day

Make sure you are getting enough carbs: (potatoes, beans, sweet potatoes, quinoa, etc.)

Drink enough water. At least half your bodyweight in ounces per day.

Eat some healthy fats each day: Hemp hearts, flax, nuts & seeds in general, avocado

Protein: You may want to at least track this at first. Aim for .6-.8 grams of protein per lb of LEAN BODYMASS. If you are 800lbs that does not mean eat 640g of protein. If that same 800lbs person got a dexa scan and found they are 130lbs of lean body mass then they should aim for 78-104g of protein. Anywhere in that range is fine. Don't overthink it.

Avoid: processed crap and also stuff that isn't food (alcohol, nicotine, caffeine, etc.).

SLEEP

For the love of god. Figure out your sleep. If you don't, this is one thing that will almost completely wreck the progress that you could be making.
