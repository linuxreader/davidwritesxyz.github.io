Sounds like CNS Fatigue. Which is very common if you are doing heavy weightlifting/powerlifting/calisthenics. Heavy lifting taxes your musculoskeletal system and your nervous system. Your body is actually damaged after you work out and needs time, rest, and proper nutrition to heal.

During the period between lifting and recovery it's completely normal to feel sluggish, brain fog, have willpower issues, sleep issues, joint pain, stiffness, etc.

Your HRV (Heart Rate Variablity) takes a dip and your resting heart rate rises during this period.

You can absolutely reduce these symptoms by doing less damage to your body during workouts. If you train to failure every set or every workout it's going to damage you more. So you could try only training to failure for your last set. You can also lower the amount of volume you are doing. Think less sets per exercises and less exercises total.

You can also train less. I highly recommend getting a Whoop (or fitbit, apple watch, w/e) and tracking your resting heart rate, HRV, and sleep for a while. Whoop specifically gives you a red, yellow, or green recovery score based on those metrics. If you are constantly in the red or yellow you will just feel like shit all the time.

I've used Whoop to figure out this issue for me. It's tempting to training hard all the time so you can really get the most results possible. But that comes at a cost.

Some people can pay that cost because training and results is everything to them. But I need the balance that lets me focus on other things in my life.

For me, walking 15k steps per day, doing a 15 minute daily morning jog, and doing only two working sets per exercise has been the sweet spot. I train every other day doing an upper-lower split. And I am in the green for every training day. And yellow on "rest days".

And I also track my nutrition to make sure I'm getting enough calories, macros/micros each day.

Over time, as you figure out how to maximize sleep, nutrition, and get in shape. You can adjust and increase your work capacity without getting so drained.
